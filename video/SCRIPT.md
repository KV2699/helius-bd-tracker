# Helius BD Tracker — narration script

**Target runtime 2:38 at 150 wpm · 396 words · cap 3:00**
Written to be spoken. Short lines. One idea per breath, because every phrase gets its own
frame and the frames are cut to word timestamps.

---

### 1 · The guess  (0:00–0:26 · 64 words)

Every business-development tool does the same three things.

It finds an announcement.

It decides the announcement means somebody needs your product.

And it hands a salesperson a lead.

That middle step is a guess.

It is usually wrong, and it is never shown to you.

*[beat]*

We built one that refuses to make it.

---

### 2 · The refusal  (0:26–0:52 · 65 words)

This is a lead scanner for Helius, who sell Solana infrastructure.

Eight agents read public pages. Five of them run on NEAR AI Cloud.

The scorer, the gate, and the veto are deliberately plain code — because a model must
never produce a number that decides anything.

And every claim has to carry a verbatim quote.

Then code searches the page we fetched for that exact quote.

If the quote is not there, the claim dies.

---

### 3 · Where the rule came from  (0:52–1:16 · 61 words)

That rule is not mine by instinct. I earned it.

In October I audited six collusion detectors with Apart Research.

Two of them could not be told apart from a crowd of deliberately meaningless rivals.

The lesson came back four separate times:

*[acid frame]* Make the detector emit the quote. And read the quote.

---

### 4 · The audit that failed us  (1:16–1:52 · 92 words)

So I pointed that standard at my own system.

The first audit scored a hundred per cent. I nearly shipped it.

It was worthless — I was testing sentences lifted straight out of the page against a
search for those sentences. A perfect score on a test you designed is a fact about the
test.

So I made it hard. I added paraphrases of real sentences, from the correct page.

*[acid frame]* Forty-nine per cent of them passed.

A model that reworded instead of quoting was getting through the check this entire
system rests on. Five hundred and seventy-three tests never caught it.

---

### 5 · The fix  (1:52–2:10 · 47 words)

The difference is what the two do to the words.

A truncation only drops words that are on the page.

A paraphrase introduces words that were never there.

So every word has to be on the page before the tolerance applies.

Real quotes kept: all of them. Paraphrases: down to two per cent.

---

### 6 · Where NEAR is  (2:10–2:30 · 48 words)

Every model call runs inside a hardware enclave on NEAR AI Cloud.

That is not a line in a README. The system fetches its own attestation —

a signing address, and a ten-thousand-character Intel quote, live.

Commercial research naming real companies is exactly the case for proving where it ran.

---

### 7 · Close  (2:30–2:38 · 19 words)

A hundred and fifty-eight companies found. Twenty-five opportunities.

Two thousand, two hundred and seventy-five rejected — every one with a reason.

*[acid]* We found our own flaw before a judge could.
