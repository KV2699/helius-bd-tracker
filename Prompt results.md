## Key Limitations and Explanation

1. **"Latency Evidence" is primarily architectural inference**: Chorus One's report explicitly states prop AMM "execution quality depends on model precision, **update latency**, and inventory limits" . This is the strongest third-party evidence linking prop AMMs to latency sensitivity. However, **no company publicly states it uses or needs Helius Sender** (consistent with the rule of "do not infer demand").

2. **First-party engineering/hiring evidence is extremely scarce**: Only Ellipsis Labs, through its Head of Trading's podcast appearance, shows first-party discussion of execution systems . No Solana prop AMM or trading firm was found with public hiring for "latency engineer," "RPC," or "transaction landing" roles.

3. **"Trading firms" are severely underrepresented**: Logic0x is the only entity explicitly described as having "Solana DEX presence + latency-sensitive arbitrage," but the source (Altss) is a directory-style profile of unknown credibility. Jump Crypto and Wintermute appear in the results but are not specifically labeled as Solana prop AMMs or latency-sensitive trading firms with detailed engineering evidence (Wintermute only corresponds to Tessera V).

4. **Recommended priority for outreach**: **Ellipsis Labs (SolFi/Phoenix)** and **HumidiFi** have the strongest evidence quality; **Wintermute (Tessera)** has brand trust, but its decision-making may be more complex; **BisonFi** and **ZeroFi** have activity evidence, but limited public information; the remaining entities (GoonFi, Aquifer, AlphaQ, Obric) are better suited as supplementary leads.

## Key Findings

**Only Drift Protocol provides A-level first-party evidence**: Its official documentation explicitly provides JIT Maker Bot and Liquidator Bot tutorials, with reference implementation `keeper-bots-v2`, and documentation mentions "track your RPC latency". This indicates Drift's ecosystem has clear automated trading infrastructure requirements, and bot operators are natural potential users of Sender.

**Titan Exchange is a B-level lead worth monitoring**: Its Prime Mode claims to optimize "transaction landing," but no engineering details are public, and its API for systematic traders is "to be released".

**BulkTrade has execution speed claims but insufficient evidence**: Third-party reports claim 5-20ms execution, but no technical documentation or GitHub, making it difficult to verify whether this relies on on-chain landing or off-chain matching.

**Mainstream DEXs (Raydium, Meteora, Orca) do not constitute Sender leads**: Their documentation only provides basic swap instruction construction and SDK examples; no keeper bots, liquidation automation, Jito, priority fee, or RPC latency references. According to the rule "DEX volume alone is monitoring, not lead," these should not be included in active outreach lists.

**Launchpad ecosystem has demand signals but no platform-level evidence**: GitHub topic shows multiple unofficial bonk.fun bundler tools using Jito and priority fees, but these are third-party developers, not official platform engineering activities; cannot confirm bonk.fun itself has infrastructure requirements.

**Recommended outreach priority**: Drift Protocol (contact bot/keeper ecosystem developers or Drift infrastructure team) > Titan Exchange (monitor its systematic trader API launch) > BulkTrade (after obtaining more technical information).
